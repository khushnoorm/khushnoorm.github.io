Number Systems (संख्या प्रणाली)

BSEB Board

1. Natural Numbers (प्राकृतिक संख्याएं)

Definition:

Natural numbers are the counting numbers starting from 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ... and so on. They are denoted by the symbol N.

N = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...}

Example:

5, 10, 25, 100, 1000 are all natural numbers.

2. Whole Numbers (पूर्ण संख्याएं)

Definition:

Whole numbers are natural numbers including zero. They are denoted by the symbol W.

W = {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...}

Example:

0, 5, 10, 25, 100 are all whole numbers.

3. Integers (पूर्णांक)

Definition:

Integers include all whole numbers and their negative counterparts. They are denoted by the symbol Z.

Z = {..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...}

Example:

-100, -5, 0, 5, 10 are all integers.

4. Rational Numbers (परिमेय संख्याएं)

Definition:

A rational number is a number that can be expressed in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0. They are denoted by the symbol Q.

Standard Form:

p/q, where p, q ∈ Z and q ≠ 0

Example:

1/2, 3/4, -5/7, 0/1 = 0, 5/1 = 5 are all rational numbers.

5. Irrational Numbers (अपरिमेय संख्याएं)

Definition:

An irrational number is a real number that cannot be expressed in the form p/q where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0. These numbers have non-terminating and non-repeating decimal representations.

Example:

π (pi), e, √2, √3, √5, √7 are irrational numbers.

π = 3.14159265358979...

√2 = 1.41421356237...

6. Real Numbers (वास्तविक संख्याएं)

Definition:

Real numbers include all rational and irrational numbers. Every point on the number line represents a real number. They are denoted by the symbol R.

R = Rational Numbers ∪ Irrational Numbers

Example:

1/2, 5, √2, π, -3.5 are all real numbers.

7. Important Formulas & Properties

Laws of Exponents:

aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
aᵐ × bᵐ = (ab)ᵐ
a⁰ = 1 (where a ≠ 0)
a⁻ᵐ = 1/aᵐ

Laws of Radicals:

√(a × b) = √a × √b
√(a/b) = √a / √b
(√a)² = a
√(aᵐ) = a^(m/2)

Rationalization:

To rationalize the denominator, multiply both numerator and denominator by the conjugate or radical.

1/√a × √a/√a = √a/a
1/(√a + √b) × (√a - √b)/(√a - √b) = (√a - √b)/(a - b)

8. Classification of Numbers

Number System Hierarchy:

Real Numbers (R)
├─ Rational Numbers (Q)
│ ├─ Integers (Z)
│ │ ├─ Whole Numbers (W)
│ │ │ └─ Natural Numbers (N)
│ │ └─ Negative Integers
│ └─ Fractions
└─ Irrational Numbers
    ├─ √2, √3, √5, ...
    ├─ π (pi)
    └─ e

9. Key Points to Remember

✓ Every rational number has a terminating or repeating decimal representation.

✓ Every irrational number has a non-terminating and non-repeating decimal representation.

✓ The decimal representation of √2 is non-terminating and non-repeating.

✓ Between any two rational numbers, there exists another rational number (Density property).

✓ The square root of a prime number is always irrational.

✓ π and e are irrational numbers.

✓ Zero is a whole number but not a natural number.

✓ All natural numbers, whole numbers, and integers are rational numbers.
